\begin{abstract}
Data systems increasingly materialize the outputs of large language models (LLMs) as tables---one row per segment
of a transcript or document, one column per extracted attribute---and answer aggregate queries over them. The rows
come from an upstream segmenter, such as a diarizer's speaker turns or a document chunker: a pipeline setting that is
usually treated as preprocessing rather than as part of the view's definition. We ask whether such LLM-derived views are
\emph{segmentation-invariant}: with exactly the same words and only the segment boundaries moved, do aggregate
answers stay the same? We show that only trivial binary columns are invariant, and measure how far real answers move with
\sys, a benchmark of re-segmentation workloads over meetings (AMI, ICSI) and documents (Wikipedia, government
reports), seven deterministic open scorers (0.5B--32B parameters, two families) and a preregistered test. Switching a
document chunker moves each article's dated-text share by a median of \nSixChunkAbsMin--\nSixChunkAbsMax{} percentage
points for every scorer, and even the sign of the shift depends on the scorer; a placebo that only moves meeting
boundaries moves the action-item share by \nSixPlaceboAbsMin--\nSixPlaceboAbsMax{} points. All three preregistered
hypotheses meet their decision rule (median relative drift \nHonepct, \nHtwopct{} and \nHfourpct\% with the primary
scorer; the first, a stress test, with a non-significant sign test), and with this scorer the placebo and chunker rules are met
again on ICSI and the government reports (post hoc). Post-hoc analyses show that part of the drift comes from the query definition
itself, while LLM scorers add a part of their own, which on documents falls with model size; that the drift exceeds
rerun noise under bf16 inference and also appears for a supervised non-LLM classifier; and that it is of the order of
a mild prompt rewording. Reusing the old rows' values saves little when a view is maintained. As a remedy (post hoc),
scoring canonical units cut from the transcript's word stream at pauses makes a meeting view invariant to boundary
moves by construction, without lowering its (modest) agreement with human labels, and lets a diarizer revision reuse
\nCnPReuseWone\% of the cached unit labels, where content hashing of segments still needs \nEWonehashshare{} of the
calls. Segmentation must be versioned with the prompt and the model, or taken out of the view definition. Code,
data pointers, cached model outputs and scripts are available at
\url{https://github.com/muxiddin19/echobase-segmentation-sensitivity}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LLM-based extraction has become a data-management operator: systems call a model once per row and store the answer
as a column, then answer analytical queries over the resulting table~\cite{evaporate,lotus,palimpzest}. When the input is
unstructured---a meeting recording, a document collection---the \emph{rows} are not given. They are produced by an
upstream segmenter: a speaker diarizer cuts audio into turns, a chunker cuts documents into passages. These segmenters
are revised often (a new diarizer release, a different chunk size), and work on maintaining LLM-derived data assumes
that rows keep their identity across revisions~\cite{semreuse,sivm}. A data system records the prompt and the model
that produced such a view, because changing either changes the answers; the segmentation is a parameter of the same
kind, but it is set by a separate pipeline stage, and nothing in the view marks two answers computed under different
segmentations as answers of different views.

This paper studies what happens when rows do not keep their identity. We fix the content exactly---the same reference words, in the same
order---and change only where segment boundaries fall. A view is \emph{segmentation-invariant} if its aggregate
query answers do not change. Fig.~\ref{fig:example} shows why invariance can fail: an utterance that proposes what the
product should have is labeled an action item as one segment but not when its last words are split off, so the
word-weighted share of action-item speech moves even though not a single word changed.

\paragraph*{Findings}
With seven deterministic open scorers and a preregistered test of the primary one:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{No scorer is segmentation-invariant.} Switching from fixed windows to paragraph chunks moves each
article's dated-text share by a median of \nSixChunkAbsMin--\nSixChunkAbsMax{} percentage points for every scorer;
the direction of the shift over the corpus depends on the scorer (median \nSixChunkSgnMin{} to \nSixChunkSgnMax{}
points), so no fixed correction undoes it. A rule-based date detector on the same chunks moves articles by
\nOrWfourRefAbs{} points. The Qwen scorers depart from it less the larger they are (median \nOrWfourExHalf{} points at
0.5B to \nOrWfourExThirtytwo{} at 32B), so this scorer-specific part shrinks with size and 32B's change follows the rule's
(Sec.~\ref{sec:dir}); the drift itself does not shrink further from 7B to 32B (\nSgWikiQdoneMsevenabsmag{} and
\nSgWikiQdoneMthirtytwoabsmag{} points). Every scorer's median relative drift is at least \nSixDMin{} on each of
the three main workloads (Table~\ref{tab:models}; cells where fewer units than the preregistered minimum pass the guard are marked there
and are descriptive).
\item \textbf{The preregistered hypotheses meet their decision rule} with the primary scorer (median relative drift): the chunker change
(\nHfourmed, \nHfourunits{} held-out articles), a placebo that moves meeting boundaries but keeps the words, segment count and
lengths (\nHtwomed), and, as a stress test, replacing diarizer turns by manual segments (\nHonemed, 16 held-out
meetings; its sign test is not significant and its absolute change is \nSgWoneQoneabsmag{} points, and at most
\nSgWoneQoneMthreeabsmag{} for the Qwen 3B--32B scorers). A small diarizer reconfiguration moves the answer by only
\nWthreeQonemed{} (exploratory).
\item \textbf{Boundaries suffice, granularity amplifies, and direction follows the predicate} (partly post-hoc).
The placebo's median drift exceeds that of the diarizer-to-manual change for all seven scorers (per meeting, clearly
for 1.5B, 7B and Phi, and for 0.5B on \nScaleN{} further meetings); merging segments raises the shares of
``any part'' predicates such as action items, by \nSgEsixkeightQoneabs{} points at $k=8$ (primary scorer), and a non-LLM
reference scorer's by a median \nOrKeightRef{} as well: the median rise is a property of the query definition (per
meeting the two still differ by a median \nOrKeightEx).
\item \textbf{Segmentation is a lever of the size of a prompt rewording} (post-hoc; primary scorer, meetings). For
action items the diarizer-to-manual drift is just below the mildest of four rewordings of the column question and the
placebo's lies within their range; both are smaller than any of the five model swaps measured (to 0.5B--14B and Phi). Showing the scorer neighboring
context does not remove the drift.
\item \textbf{The effects recur} (post-hoc). With the primary scorer the placebo and chunker rules are met again on a
second corpus per domain (ICSI meetings, \nIcPWtwoAcD; government reports, \nCrsPD); with 3B the chunker rule is met
(\nCrsTD) and the placebo rule fails because too few ICSI meetings have a measurable action-item share; with 0.5B
both are met (\nIcHWtwoAcD, \nCrsHD); segmentation drift exceeds the rerun noise of bf16
inference on \nBfAll{} workloads; and a supervised TF-IDF classifier also drifts (descriptive; it did not reach our validity bar).
\item \textbf{Maintenance gains little from the old rows} (exploratory). After re-segmentation, the overlapping old
row's value is wrong too often for a certified audit to accept it (\nEWonehashshare{} of the calls remain, exactly as
for content hashing); structural rules lower this to \nEWoneechobaseepsonezeroshare{} at a 10\% cell-error tolerance.
\item \textbf{Canonical units remove boundary drift} (post-hoc). Scoring units cut from the words at pauses, not from
the segmenter, gives exactly the same answer under the placebo and every merge without lowering agreement with
human labels, and after a diarizer revision \nCnPReuseWone\% of the new version's units reuse a cached label
(Sec.~\ref{sec:canon}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Segment-Keyed LLM Views}\label{sec:model}
\begin{definition}[Segmentation]
Let $w = (w_1,\dots,w_n)$ be the words of a source (a meeting or a document) with speakers and times. A
\emph{segmentation} $S$ partitions the word positions into segments $s_1,\dots,s_k$; each segment has a text (its words
in order) and, for speech, a speaker.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[LLM-derived view]
For a column $c$ with answer set $A_c$ and a scorer $f_c$, the view $V_c(S)$ has one row per segment $s\in S$ with value
$f_c(\mathrm{text}(s))\in A_c$. Our scorer is deterministic: the first-token argmax over $A_c$.
\end{definition}
\begin{definition}[Query and drift]
A word-weighted share query $Q_{c,a}(S) = \sum_{s\in S:\,f_c(s)=a}|s| \,/\, \sum_{s\in S}|s|$ does not depend on the
number of segments. For two segmentations $S_{\mathrm{old}},S_{\mathrm{new}}$ of the same words, the drift of a unit
(meeting or article) is $D = |Q(S_{\mathrm{new}}) - Q(S_{\mathrm{old}})| / Q(S_{\mathrm{old}})$, defined when
$Q(S_{\mathrm{old}})\ge 0.02$. A view is \emph{segmentation-invariant} for $Q$ if $Q(S)$ is the same for all segmentations $S$ of $w$.
\end{definition}
Invariance is a strong property. For a single word stream and segmentations into contiguous segments, only trivial
binary columns have it:
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:trivial}
Let $f$ be a binary column and $Q=Q_{f,\mathrm{yes}}$. $Q(S)$ is the same for every segmentation $S$ of $w$ into
contiguous segments if and only if $f$ gives the same answer to every contiguous span of $w$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
($\Leftarrow$) $Q\in\{0,1\}$ for every $S$. ($\Rightarrow$) Let $Y$ be the words labeled yes as singletons, so
$Q(S_1)=|Y|/n$ for the all-singleton segmentation $S_1$. If $\emptyset\ne Y\ne w$, some adjacent pair $(w_i,w_{i+1})$
has exactly one word in $Y$; merging only that pair gives $Q=(|Y|\pm1)/n\ne Q(S_1)$. If $Y=\emptyset$ and some span $s$
is labeled yes, the segmentation with $s$ and singletons elsewhere gives $Q=|s|/n>0$; $Y=w$ is symmetric.
\end{proof}
For any LLM column that answers differently for different texts, some stream and some pair of segmentations
therefore change the answer; invariance cannot be assumed. The question
this paper answers is empirical: \emph{how much} do aggregate answers move when the segmentation changes in ways
that occur in practice (replacing system turns by curated ones, switching or resizing a chunker, reconfiguring a diarizer) or in
a controlled way (a placebo, a merge), and how much of that movement granularity explains? Structural rules (Sec.~\ref{sec:rq4}) are the
special cases where a new segment's value follows from its parts, e.g., a segment whose parts are all ``no'' for a
predicate that is monotone under concatenation.

\section{Benchmark}\label{sec:bench}
\paragraph*{Data}
AMI Meeting Corpus~\cite{ami} (Mix-Headset audio, manual annotations 1.6.2): 18 development meetings and 16 held-out
meetings (the standard full-corpus-ASR test list, which is also AMI's standard diarization test list~\cite{amisetup}).
AMI is distributed under CC BY 4.0; we collected no new data from people, and speakers appear only under the
corpus's anonymous letters. Wikipedia (English dump 2023-11-01, first of the 41 shards of the Hugging Face
\texttt{wikimedia/wikipedia} release, CC BY-SA 3.0 and GFDL): 200 development and 200 held-out articles of
800--3,000 words, sampled with a fixed seed. Post hoc (D17), a second corpus per domain: 20 of the 75 ICSI
meetings~\cite{icsi} with their transcription segments and MRDA dialogue acts~\cite{mrda}, and 200 of the 362 CRS
test reports of GovReport~\cite{govreport}, each drawn with a fixed seed.
\paragraph*{Workloads} All workloads keep the words fixed (up to the \nDropWone\% of words outside diarizer turns, below);
only the segmentation changes.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{W1, diarizer $\to$ manual.} pyannote 3.1~\cite{pyannote} turns with the known speaker count, filled with
the AMI reference words, versus the AMI manual segments.
\item \textbf{W2, placebo.} From the manual segmentation, per speaker: the same word order, the same number of segments
and the same multiset of segment lengths, with lengths shuffled (seed per meeting). Granularity is matched; only
boundary placement moves.
\item \textbf{W3, speaker count (exploratory).} pyannote 3.1 with an estimated versus the known speaker count.
\item \textbf{W4, chunker.} Fixed 120-word windows versus paragraphs merged forward to at least 40 words.
\item \textbf{E6, granularity.} Manual segments merged in windows of $k\in\{2,4,8\}$ consecutive same-speaker segments.
\end{itemize}
\paragraph*{Same words on both sides} Every version of a meeting is built from the same AMI reference words, so
speech-recognition errors cannot differ between versions. A system segmentation (diarizer turns) is filled by
assigning each reference word to the turn that contains its midpoint; system speakers are mapped to reference
speakers by a maximum-overlap assignment. The placebo and the merges contain exactly the same words in the same order
as the manual segments and differ only in how they are grouped; a diarizer version also leaves out the words whose
midpoint lies in no turn (\nDropWoneN{} of \nDropTotal{} held-out words, \nDropWone\%, under W1; \nDropWthree\% for the
estimated speaker count of W3). For documents, both
chunkers cut the same article text.
\paragraph*{Columns and queries} Meetings: action item and question (yes/no), entity count (0--9), sentiment (3
labels), topic (5 labels). Documents: date mention (yes/no), entity count, topic (7 labels). Queries over meetings
are word-weighted shares: Q1 action items (primary), Q2 questions, Q3 negative sentiment, Q4 topic = cost; plus Q5,
the relative change of the entity sum, and Q6, the total variation distance between topic distributions. Documents
use Qd1, the dated share of text (primary), Qd2, the entity sum, and Qd3, the topic distance. Word weighting makes the
shares independent of how many segments a version has, so a coarser segmentation is not penalized for having fewer
rows.
\paragraph*{Scoring} Each cell is one model call: a system instruction naming the answer format, the column's
question and the segment text, followed by ``Answer:''. The value is the argmax of the next-token logits restricted
to the first tokens of the allowed answers, so there is no sampling and no parsing. The gap between the top two
logits is kept as a confidence score. Calls are batched by length under a fixed token budget. We use
Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25} (primary) and Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (second), in fp32 with pinned revisions, on
two NVIDIA TITAN Xp (12\,GB) cards. Before the held-out runs, rescoring the same development inputs under three batch
budgets gave identical labels; after them, rescoring \nDetN{} held-out segments under three budgets changed
\nDetMism{} labels in the three columns checked (action item, entity count and topic). Within this setup (model, precision, hardware),
drift is therefore a function of the segmentation, not sampling noise; determinism is not automatic, since a bf16 run
of the 7B model failed the same check (Sec.~\ref{sec:rq3}). The 1.5B model scores \nThrBig{} segments per second on
one card (the 0.5B model \nThrSmall); a full five-column view of the 16 held-out meetings is \nFullCalls{} calls,
about \nFullMin{} minutes. A second model was admitted before the freeze only if its development yes-rate for the
two binary columns lay in [0.05, 0.60], so that drift of a share is measurable; a 1.7B model from another family
answered ``yes'' almost never (0.000 and 0.002) and was not admitted, and the planned fallback, Qwen2.5-0.5B, was. The
post-hoc models (3B, 7B, 14B, 32B and Phi-3.5-mini, Sec.~\ref{sec:rq3}) were not subject to this rule. Prompts were
designed on development data with the primary model and applied unchanged to every other scorer.

\paragraph*{Protocol} We separated exploration from confirmation. All design work used 18 development meetings and
200 development articles. Before any held-out unit was scored, we froze the hypotheses, thresholds, unit lists,
prompts, model revisions and analysis code in a preregistration, and recorded the SHA-256 of every file that can
affect a score; the evaluation scripts check for a recorded freeze before touching held-out data, and the hashes were
re-checked before the analysis. One defect in the freeze check (it accepted an older marker) was fixed after the
hashes were first written; the fix was logged as a deviation and the hashes rewritten before any held-out unit was
scored, and the confirmatory analysis ran once. The confirmatory family has three hypotheses, tested in the fixed sequence H1$\to$H2$\to$H4: a
hypothesis is claimed only if all earlier ones are supported. Each uses the median drift over units with a 95\% bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa)
bootstrap interval~\cite{bca} (10,000 resamples over units, fixed seed); it is \emph{supported} when the median
reaches a threshold $T$ and the interval's lower end reaches $L$, \emph{refuted} when the upper end is below $T$, and
inconclusive otherwise. Thresholds were set after the development drifts were seen, below them so that an effect of
the development size passes: $T=0.10$ for H1 and H4 (development medians \nDevHonemed{} and \nDevHfourmed) and $T=0.05$
for H2 (\nDevHtwomed),\footnote{The development and held-out medians of H2 coincide to three decimals; the 18
development and 16 held-out meetings are disjoint, and their per-meeting drifts differ.} a minimal-effect threshold for
a placebo; the development results are not evidence for the
hypotheses. Since Proposition~\ref{prop:trivial} already rules out invariance in general, the hypotheses are claims about
magnitude, not existence: that drift on real data reaches a size a consumer would notice; the medians and intervals
are the result. Simulated power of the exact rules from the development units was \nPowerHone{} (H1), \nPowerHtwo{} (H2)
and \nPowerHfour{} (H4). The analysis ran once. Two planned workloads failed their development checks before the
freeze (a release comparison, pyannote 3.0 versus 3.1, could not run because 3.0 jobs reset our host, and a
clustering-threshold variant of 3.1 changed only 0.4\% of segments),
so the diarizer-revision hypothesis was dropped and W3 became exploratory; these deviations were logged before any
held-out data were touched. All post-hoc analyses were added after the confirmatory analysis in twelve rounds (D5--D9, D11, D12, D14, D15, D17--D19), each prompted
by an internal review of the draft (see the declaration at the end) and logged with its settings and decision rules
before it ran; they are marked ``post-hoc'' where they appear. All logs are released (except the partial cache of a
discarded bf16 7B run, deleted unanalyzed). The hashes are an internal record, not a third-party timestamp.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 6b. Direction and correctness (post-hoc, D5)
\section{Direction and Correctness}\label{sec:dir}
The analyses in this section were added after the confirmatory analysis, following an internal review; they are
post-hoc and exploratory (logged as a deviation before they were run).
\paragraph*{Drift has a direction when the re-segmentation is systematic} Relative drift $D$ is unsigned. Signed,
absolute changes separate erratic from systematic movement. Merging segments raises the yes-share of predicates that
are satisfied by \emph{any} part of a segment: at $k=8$ the action-item share rises by a median
\nSgEsixkeightQoneabs{} percentage points (in \nSgEsixkeightQoneup{} of 16 meetings) and the question share by
\nSgEsixkeightQtwoabs{} (\nSgEsixkeightQtwoup{} of 16), while the share of negative sentiment, a judgment about the
whole segment, falls by \nSgEsixkeightQthreeabs{} (rising in only \nSgEsixkeightQthreeup{} of 16) (merges were scored
with the primary model only). The placebo, which cuts utterances at arbitrary words, changes the question share by
\nSgWtwoQtwoabs{} points with the primary model, and the question share falls clearly for three of the seven scorers (it rises
in \nSgWtwoQtwoup, \nSgWtwoQtwoMtwoup{} and \nSgWtwoQtwoMthreeup{} of 16 meetings for the 1.5B, 0.5B and 3B models) and
less clearly for Phi (\nSgWtwoQtwoMphiabs{} points, interval \nSgWtwoQtwoMphiabslo{} to \nSgWtwoQtwoMphiabshi; rising
in \nSgWtwoQtwoMphiup{} of 16): a question split across two segments is often recognized in neither. The three
largest models are the exceptions: the question share rises by \nSgWtwoQtwoMsevenabs{} points for 7B
(\nSgWtwoQtwoMsevenabslo{} to \nSgWtwoQtwoMsevenabshi; in \nSgWtwoQtwoMsevenup{} of 16) and by
\nSgWtwoQtwoMfourteenabs{} for 14B (\nSgWtwoQtwoMfourteenabslo{} to \nSgWtwoQtwoMfourteenabshi; in
\nSgWtwoQtwoMfourteenup{} of 16) and by \nSgWtwoQtwoMthirtytwoabs{} for 32B (\nSgWtwoQtwoMthirtytwoabslo{} to
\nSgWtwoQtwoMthirtytwoabshi; in \nSgWtwoQtwoMthirtytwoup{} of 16), as if they recognized a question in both halves. Larger scorers, up to 32B, therefore do not
remove the effect; they can reverse its direction. For action
items the direction depends on the model: under the placebo the share rises in \nSixPlaceboUpMin{} to
\nSixPlaceboUpMax{} of 16 meetings, depending on the scorer. Replacing diarizer turns by manual segments (W1) has no clear direction on the 16 held-out meetings
(\nSgWoneQoneabs{} points, interval \nSgWoneQoneabslo{} to \nSgWoneQoneabshi; rising in \nSgWoneQoneup{} of 16; see the
scale run below), and
paragraph chunking raises the dated share of articles by \nSgWikiQdoneabs{} points with the primary model (rising in
\nSgWikiQdoneup{} of 200) but lowers it by \nSgWikiQdoneMfourteenabs{} with 14B (\nSgWikiQdoneMfourteenabslo{} to
\nSgWikiQdoneMfourteenabshi), so a correction factor learned for one scorer would not transfer to another. Segmentation can therefore bias LLM-derived aggregates: with the primary model, coarsening raised predicates satisfied by
any part of a segment, while the direction under arbitrary cuts depends on the predicate and the scorer.
\paragraph*{How much is the query definition? (post-hoc, D14)} A word-weighted share of an ``any part'' predicate
changes with segmentation even under a scorer that never errs on its own terms (Proposition~\ref{prop:mono}), so part
of the drift is a unit-of-aggregation effect~\cite{maup} of the query, not of the LLM. To separate the two, we applied
deterministic reference scorers to exactly the segments the LLMs scored: on documents, a rule that labels a chunk
dated if it contains a year or a month name; on meetings, a rule that labels a segment ``yes'' if any of its words
lies in a human dialogue act of the column's type. Neither is ground truth for the LLM columns. The reference already
moves: the chunker change moves an article's dated share by a median \nOrWfourRefAbs{} points (signed
\nOrWfourRefSgn), W1 moves the action-item and question shares by \nOrWoneRefAc{} and \nOrWoneRefQu, the placebo by
\nOrWtwoRefAc{} and \nOrWtwoRefQu, and merging $k=8$ segments raises the action-item share by a median \nOrKeightRef{}
points, as the scorer's \nOrKeightLlm: the median merge effect belongs to the query definition, although per meeting the
two differ by a median \nOrKeightEx{} points (\nOrKeightExLo--\nOrKeightExHi). Elsewhere the LLMs depart
from the reference: per unit, the median of $|\Delta_{\mathrm{LLM}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}|$ is
\nOrAmiExMin--\nOrAmiExMax{} points on W1 and the placebo for every scorer and both columns, and
\nOrWfourExMinSix--\nOrWfourExMax{} on the chunker change for the six scorers up to 14B, each interval above 1 point;
for these six, LLM and reference changes are only weakly correlated on documents (Spearman
\nOrWfourRhoMin--\nOrWfourRhoMaxSix, highest for 14B). On documents the departure falls with size within the Qwen
family (\nOrWfourExQwenSeq{} points from 0.5B to 32B; Phi \nOrWfourExPhi), and at 32B the chunker change follows the
reference (signed \nOrWfourSgnLlmThirtytwo{} against \nOrWfourSgnRefThirtytwo{} points, Spearman
\nOrWfourRhoThirtytwo) and departs from it by only \nOrWfourExThirtytwo{} (interval
\nOrWfourExThirtytwoLo--\nOrWfourExThirtytwoHi): at this size the document drift that remains is mostly definitional. On
meetings there is no such trend for action items (placebo: \nOrWtwoAcExQwenSeq{} points from 0.5B to 32B), only for
questions (\nOrWtwoQuExQwenSeq), and 32B departs from the reference in every meeting cell. These are six sizes of one
family; we claim nothing beyond 32B. The small diarizer
reconfiguration W3 barely moves the reference (\nOrWthreeRef{} points), and the LLM departs from it by
\nOrWthreeEx{} (interval from \nOrWthreeExLo). Segmentation drift thus has two parts: a definitional one, which
follows from the predicate and the change (merges raise ``any part'' shares), and a scorer-specific one whose size
and sign differ across scorers and which, on documents, larger scorers reduce.
\paragraph*{Drift changes agreement with human labels} AMI's manual dialogue acts give a human reference for two
columns: questions (elicit acts) and, as a proxy for action items, suggestions; both type sets were fixed in an earlier
pilot. Table~\ref{tab:gold} compares each segmentation's word-weighted share with the human share. The scorer
over-predicts both predicates under every segmentation (its notion of a question is broader than an elicit act; the
human question share is \nGoldQushare\%). Across the segmentations a pipeline would produce (manual, diarizer turns,
estimated speaker count) the question-share error varies modestly, from \nGoldQumanualabserr{} to
\nGoldQuwoneoldabserr{} points; across coarser segmentations it grows to \nGoldQumergeeightabserr{} points; across
scorers on the same manual segments it ranges from \nGoldQumanualabserrMphi{} (Phi-3.5-mini) to
\nGoldQumanualabserr{} (1.5B).
Lower share error is not better segmentation: the placebo looks closest only because it pushes shares down. Word-level
F1, which does not reward such compensation, is \nGoldQumanualfone{} for manual segments and \nGoldQuwoneoldfone{} for
diarizer turns on questions with the primary model, and \nGoldQumanualfoneMthree{} under each of the three
segmentations scored with the 3B model. These F1 values must be read against a ceiling (post-hoc, D17): word-level F1
credits every word of a ``yes'' segment while a dialogue act covers only some of them, so a labeler that is right

\paragraph*{A scale for drift} To read a drift of 0.15, we measure the same statistic for two changes that pipelines
already treat as versioned, on the \emph{fixed} manual segmentation of the held-out meetings. Four rewordings of each
column question, with the same answer sets and scoring, change the action-item share by a median \nCalParaDQonemed,
\nCalParaQonemed, \nCalParaBQonemed{} and \nCalParaCQonemed{}, and the question share by \nCalParaBQtwomed{} to
\nCalParaCQtwomed{} (absolute \nCalParaBQtwoabs{} to \nCalParaCQtwoabs{} points: what counts as a question shifts with
its wording). The first rewording with the 0.5B scorer changes the action-item share by \nCalParaHalfQonemed{}
against a W1 drift of \nWoneQoneMtwomed{} for that model. Replacing the 1.5B scorer by the 0.5B one changes the action-item share by
\nCalSwapHalfQonemed, by Phi-3.5-mini or 3B by \nCalSwapPhiQonemed{} and \nCalSwapThreeQonemed{} (all on the same
cards), and by 7B or 14B by \nCalSwapSevenQonemed{} and \nCalSwapFourteenQonemed{} (other hardware, on which each
reproduced the labels checked, Sec.~\ref{sec:rq3}). W1 drift (\nHonemed{} for action items,
\nWoneQtwomed{} for questions) is thus just below the mildest rewording for action items, and about a fifth of the
mildest for questions; the placebo's action-item drift (\nHtwomed) lies within the range of the rewordings, and both
segmentation drifts are smaller than any of the five model swaps measured. Segmentation is a smaller lever than the prompt, but for action items of the
same order as its mildest rewordings. Prompts and models are routinely recorded with an LLM-derived
result; the segmentation usually is not.
\paragraph*{Context padding is not a fix} A natural remedy is to let the scorer see the neighbors of each segment:
we show the two preceding and two following segments as context and label only the target, as DocETL's gather
operator does for split documents~\cite{docetl}. This costs
\nCtxRatio$\times$ the input tokens (\nCtxTokCtx{} instead of \nCtxTokPlain{} per call). It lowers W1 drift (action
items \nWoneQoneCtxmed{} instead of \nHonemed; questions \nWoneQtwoCtxmed{} instead of \nWoneQtwomed) and placebo drift
for questions (\nWtwoQtwoCtxmed{} instead of \nWtwoQtwomed), but raises placebo drift for action items to
\nWtwoQoneCtxmed{}, and it reduces agreement with the human labels (question F1 \nGoldQumanualfoneCtx{} instead of
\nGoldQumanualfone{} on manual segments) because the model then labels more of the speech as questions. Context
changes what the view measures as much as it stabilizes it; a segmentation-robust view needs more than a wider prompt.
\paragraph*{Structure, formally} Structural reuse would be sound for predicates that compose over segments.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mono} 
Let $f$ be a binary column that is monotone under concatenation: $f(st) \ge \max(f(s), f(t))$ with yes $>$ no. If
$S'$ coarsens $S$ (every segment of $S'$ is a concatenation of consecutive segments of $S$), then
$Q_{f,\mathrm{yes}}(S') \ge Q_{f,\mathrm{yes}}(S)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A word labeled yes under $S$ lies in a segment $s$ with $f(s)=$ yes; its segment under $S'$ contains $s$, so it is
labeled yes as well. Word weights do not change.
\end{proof}
Action items and questions are intended to be such ``any part'' predicates, and merging indeed raises their shares
(Sec.~\ref{sec:dir}). But the scorer violates monotonicity often: among merged segments with at least two parts,
\nMonoktwoAcloss\% (at $k=2$) to \nMonokeightAcloss\% (at $k=8$) are labeled ``no'' although one of their parts was
labeled ``yes'' (questions: \nMonoktwoQuloss\% to \nMonokeightQuloss\%). The rules of Sec.~\ref{sec:rq4} therefore
cannot be trusted without verification, and reusing the overlapping old value is no better: it is right for only
\nAnchorAccactionitem\% of changed action-item cells, \nAnchorAccquestion\% of question cells and
\nAnchorAcctopic\% of topic cells.

\section{RQ4: Consequences for View Maintenance}\label{sec:rq4}
When a segmenter is revised, a view must be brought up to date. Recomputing every changed row is always correct; the
question is how much of that cost can be avoided without returning wrong cells. We replay five policies on the cached
outputs of W1 (\nEWonecells{} changed cells over 16 held-out meetings and five columns), so every policy's LLM calls
and its true cell error are known exactly.
\paragraph*{Policies}
\emph{Full} recomputes every changed cell. \emph{Content hash} reuses a value only when the new segment's text equals
an old segment's text; this is exact. \emph{Anchor + audit} is our adaptation of the stable-row reuse of prior work~\cite{sivm,semreuse}, which was not
designed for re-segmentation and which we did not run:
it gives each new cell the value of its most-overlapping old segment (its anchor) as a provisional value and certifies
it per column with an exact hypergeometric audit. \emph{Rules/anchor + audit} proposes values from structural
rules where they apply (e.g., a segment whose parts are all ``no'' for a monotone predicate is ``no''; an entity count
of a segment that is the exact union of old segments is their sum) and from anchors elsewhere, and audits each
provenance stratum. The audit samples a stratum with doubling sample sizes and certifies it when the $(1-\alpha)$ upper
bound on its number of wrong provisional values is at most $\epsilon N$; an uncertifiable stratum is recomputed; the
family error $\delta=0.05$ is split over strata (union bound). A further variant splits strata by the scorer's
confidence on the old cells.

\nPoolWoneanchorerr\% of them (\nPoolWthreeanchorerr\% under W3); no audit can certify reuse at that error rate.

The smaller the revision, the more of the view survives by content alone.

calls on the real meetings and \nScalEchoshareRep{} on the largest set, against \nScalHashshare{} for hashing.

random sample of the changed cells, with or without the old rows as a proxy~\cite{blazeit,abae,ppi}, does not help

by the rule fixed before the analysis neither estimator certifies a meeting's answer to within 2 points for fewer
calls than hashing.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 8b. Canonical units (post-hoc, D18, D19)
\section{Canonical Units: Invariance by Construction}\label{sec:canon}
The lesson so far is to version the segmenter with the view. A scoring operator can instead take the segmenter out of
the view definition (post-hoc, D18; second scorer D19).
\begin{definition}[Canonical units]
For a fixed word stream whose words carry speakers and times from the transcript (not from any segmentation) and a
gap $g$, the canonical units are, per speaker, the maximal runs of that
speaker's words in time order in which no two consecutive words are $g$ or more seconds apart. The canonical view of a
segmentation $S$ labels the canonical units of the words $S$ contains, and a share query is word-weighted over these
units.
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:canon}
Two segmentations that contain the same words, with the same speakers and times, have the same canonical view, so
every query over it has the same answer.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The units and their texts are functions of the words, speakers and times alone, and a deterministic scorer (or a
label cache keyed by text) gives a text one label.
\end{proof}
This does not contradict Proposition~\ref{prop:trivial}: the operator does not score the segmenter's rows at all, so
a canonical view is a different view, not a corrected segment view; its unit, like a segment, still decides what a
label means. We fixed $g=0.5$\,s from development pause statistics without labels, scored the units of every version of
the held-out meetings with the frozen prompts, and cached labels by unit text, so a unit that recurs in another version
is scored once. The rule fixed before any label: the operator keeps validity for a column if its word-level F1 against
the dialogue acts is at least 0.9 times that of the same scorer on manual segments.

gap, and the gap and the source of word times and speakers are themselves parameters of the view: changing them is a
re-segmentation. Agreement with human labels stays as low as with segments (the rule only protects it). A canonical
view changes what a row is, so it must be compared only with canonical views.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 9. Related work (0.75 pp)
\section{Related Work}
\paragraph*{Semantic operators and LLM-derived data} Generating structured views of unstructured data with LLMs is an
established data-management task: Evaporate builds queryable tables from heterogeneous document collections and
trades extraction quality against LLM cost~\cite{evaporate}. Semantic data systems execute LLM calls as query
operators: LOTUS defines declarative semantic operators with accuracy guarantees~\cite{lotus}, Palimpzest optimizes
AI analytics for cost, runtime and quality~\cite{palimpzest}, DocETL rewrites document-processing pipelines with an
agent~\cite{docetl}, ZenDB answers SQL over templated documents using their structure~\cite{zendb}, and Stretto is an
execution engine for such systems~\cite{stretto}; SemBench benchmarks such engines for quality and cost across
scenarios, modalities and operators~\cite{sembench}, and UDA-Bench evaluates LLM-powered systems for unstructured-data
analysis against manually annotated relational views~\cite{udabench}; both compare systems, while we hold the system
and the words fixed and move only the segment boundaries. All of them operate on rows that an upstream step has already
produced (tuples, documents, chunks) or derive the units from document structure~\cite{zendb}; we are not aware
of one that measures how query answers depend on that choice of units. Recent work maintains their results under change: SemReuse certifies the reuse of cached
natural-language views for new semantic predicates over the same tuples~\cite{semreuse}, and sIVM certifies the
maintenance of LLM-computed cells under prompt edits~\cite{sivm}. Both target other kinds of change and key results by
a stable row identity. We study the change they assume away: the rows themselves move while the content stays fixed, and we show that this alone
changes the answers.
\paragraph*{View maintenance, provenance and evolving text} Materialized-view maintenance avoids recomputation by
detecting updates that cannot affect a view and re-evaluating the rest differentially~\cite{blakeley86}; it is now
part of industrial data-engineering pipelines~\cite{enzyme}, and for embedding-based semantic indexes a synthetic
study finds incremental maintenance cost to follow the size of the change rather than of the corpus~\cite{costchange}. Its correctness rests on the view being a function of
the base data. For LLM-derived views this holds only once the segmentation is fixed, so we treat the segmenter as part
of the view definition (Sec.~\ref{sec:model}) and measure what maintenance costs when it changes
(Sec.~\ref{sec:rq4}). Our structural rules and anchors are a form of where-provenance~\cite{whywhere}: they map a
new segment to the old segments its words came from. For rule-based extraction over updated documents, Kassaie and Tompa treat extracted relations as materialized
views and identify updates after which they can be maintained without re-extraction~\cite{kassaie23}, and Cyclex
recycles extraction results across snapshots of evolving text~\cite{cyclex}; both target extractors whose outputs are
tied to text regions. Our audit, like approximate selection with guarantees~\cite{supg}, bounds error from a sample of
exact labels (here: recomputed cells), and our sampled estimate of an answer (D12) uses the old rows as a proxy in the
manner of proxy-assisted aggregation~\cite{blazeit,abae} and prediction-powered inference~\cite{ppi}; what is new is
that the proxy is a stale view under another segmentation, and its variance is too large to certify a meeting's
answer. LLM extractors read a whole
segment, so the segment boundary is part of the input; Propositions~\ref{prop:trivial} and~\ref{prop:mono} make this
precise.
\paragraph*{Versioning} Dataset versioning systems version the rows of a table~\cite{decibel,orpheusdb}, and
model-lifecycle systems record models with their parameters and data~\cite{modelhub}. For an LLM-derived view, the
segmenter that produces the rows belongs to the version as well: our results show that the same words under two
segmentations are two different views.
\paragraph*{Sensitivity of LLMs} NLP work shows that LLM outputs change under semantically equivalent prompt
formats~\cite{sclar}, and LLM-based extraction from clinical notes shifts with the prompt, the model and the
schema~\cite{murin}; segmentation is the factor these studies hold fixed. We hold the prompt fixed and change only how the data are cut, a decision made by a data
pipeline, and measure the effect on aggregate query answers rather than on single outputs.
\paragraph*{Segmenters} Speaker diarization~\cite{pyannote} and document chunking decide the rows of segment-keyed
views; they are evaluated by their own error metrics, not by the stability of downstream analytical answers. In
meeting processing, dialog-act classification is known to degrade with automatic segmentation and recognized words
rather than human ones~\cite{angdialog}; there the words change too and the target is per-segment accuracy, while we
hold the words fixed and measure aggregate query answers. In
retrieval-augmented generation, chunking is tuned for answer quality: a systematic study finds that overlap brings no
benefit and that sentence chunking is the most cost-effective method~\cite{chunkqa}. We ask a different question:
not which chunking answers best, but how much an aggregate changes when the chunking changes.
\paragraph*{Unit-of-aggregation effects} That aggregates depend on the units over which data are grouped is known in
spatial statistics as the modifiable areal unit problem~\cite{maup}. Our setting differs in where the dependence
arises: the measurement itself (the LLM's label of a unit) changes with the unit, not only the aggregation, and the
direction follows the predicate (Sec.~\ref{sec:dir}).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 8. Discussion (0.75 pp)
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:disc}
\paragraph*{No segmentation is the right one} Neither version is, and the claim does not need one. Manual segments are a reference for
diarization quality, not for what an LLM should answer, and the placebo is deliberately arbitrary. The finding is that
the answer depends on a choice that pipelines make for unrelated reasons (speaker change detection, index chunk size),
so the choice must be recorded and held fixed wherever answers are compared.
\paragraph*{Not sampling noise} The scorer is deterministic (Sec.~\ref{sec:bench}), so every difference between two
versions is caused by the segmentation. Rescoring held-out segments under different batch budgets, and on a second
server, reproduced every label checked (Sec.~\ref{sec:bench}, Sec.~\ref{sec:dir}). Deployed inference is often
nondeterministic, so we also ran the primary scorer in bf16 under two batch budgets (post-hoc, D17; emulated on our
Pascal cards): \nBfFlipMin--\nBfFlipMax\% of labels change between the two runs, and per unit the segmentation change
exceeds this rerun change on \nBfAll{} workloads (median per-unit rerun change at most \nBfRerunMax{} points, segmentation
change at least \nBfSegMin; e.g., placebo questions \nBfWtwoQuSeg{} against \nBfWtwoQuRerun; the narrowest margin is for W1 questions, \nBfWoneQuSeg{} against \nBfWoneQuRerun). Drift
is not hidden by this rerun noise.
\paragraph*{A kind of input sensitivity} In part, the segment text is the model's input, so segmentation
sensitivity is a kind of input sensitivity. What distinguishes it from prompt sensitivity~\cite{sclar} is that the
instruction and the content of the source are fixed and only the unit the question is asked about changes, and that
unit is chosen by a data pipeline, not by the user. That makes it a property of the view definition, which a data
system can record, version and test.
\paragraph*{Threats to validity} \emph{Internal:} versions are built from reference words, which removes automatic speech recognition (ASR) error but
also removes a realistic source of change; diarizer turns are mapped to reference speakers by overlap, so a mapping
error could add drift, but the placebo, which needs no mapping, drifts more. pyannote's segmentation model was trained
on the training sets of nine corpora including AMI~\cite{segcard}; our held-out meetings form AMI's standard
diarization test list~\cite{amisetup}, on which its authors report results~\cite{bredin23}. \emph{External:} the
confirmatory tests use one 1.5B model; a 0.5B model (preregistered, exploratory) and five post-hoc scorers up to 32B,
one from another family, extend them, but none of the large hosted models; on documents the scorer-specific part
of the drift shrinks with size up to 32B (Sec.~\ref{sec:dir}), so larger models may leave mostly the definitional part.
Determinism was obtained in fp32 and checked per scorer and host on a subset of columns; hosted APIs give no such
guarantee, but in our bf16 check rerun noise stays below the drift on every workload. Prompts were designed with the primary model
and not tuned for the others, so we compare drift within each scorer, not label levels across scorers;
the second corpus per domain was added post hoc, with one or two scorers; English only. We release the harness so that further models and domains can be measured.
\emph{Construct:} our queries are word-weighted shares, entity sums and topic distances;
queries over segment counts would move more, since segment counts change by construction. Every scorer agrees only
weakly with human labels (question F1 at most \nGoldQumanualfoneMseven, against a ceiling of \nCeilQu{} for any
segment labeler), so drift is measured on labels of limited validity; it persists over confident labels
(Sec.~\ref{sec:rq3}) and for a supervised classifier (Sec.~\ref{sec:dir}). \emph{Statistical:} the confirmatory
tests use 16 meetings, four sessions of each of four series, so their intervals are wide and the meetings are not
independent; H1 and H2 hold with any one series left out (smallest medians \nLosoHoneMin{} and \nLosoHtwoMin, post-hoc,
D14); the post-hoc scale runs on \nScaleN{} further meetings (0.5B and
1.5B models) give narrower intervals and, for 1.5B, a larger W1 drift (\nScalePWoneQonemed{} against \nHonemed), on a
different population and without preregistration. Post-hoc intervals are not adjusted for multiplicity, and the
post-hoc rounds were chosen in response to reviews.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 9. Lessons (0.5 pp)
\section{Lessons for System Builders}\label{sec:lessons}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \textbf{The segmenter is part of the view definition.} Version it with the prompt and the model; an answer
computed under one segmentation is not comparable to one computed under another.
\item \textbf{Test invariance before trusting a pipeline.} A placebo re-segmentation (Sec.~\ref{sec:bench}) costs one
extra scoring pass and shows how much of an answer is an artifact of boundaries; a cheap rule-based reference scorer
on the same segments separates the part that the query definition causes (Sec.~\ref{sec:dir}).
\item \textbf{Reuse by content, or audit.} When boundaries move, reusing a cell is exact only for identical text;
anything else needs verification (Sec.~\ref{sec:rq4}).
\item \textbf{Or take the segmenter out of the view.} Scoring content-defined units, such as pause-delimited runs of
one speaker, makes the answer invariant to boundary moves and lets a segmenter revision reuse most cached labels;
the unit definition is then the versioned parameter (Sec.~\ref{sec:canon}).
\item \textbf{Report the unit.} Drift is heterogeneous across meetings and documents; report per-unit distributions,
not one pooled number.
\end{enumerate}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- 10. Conclusion (0.5 pp)
\section{Conclusion}
LLM-derived views inherit the segmentation of their source. Holding every word fixed, switching a document chunker
moved each article's answer by a median of \nSixChunkAbsMin--\nSixChunkAbsMax{} percentage points for each of seven
scorers, in a direction that depends on the scorer, and a placebo that only moves meeting boundaries moved the
action-item share by \nSixPlaceboAbsMin--\nSixPlaceboAbsMax{} points; all three preregistered hypotheses met their
decision rule. Segmentation is therefore part of the definition of such a view, with an effect of the order of a prompt
rewording for the primary scorer, and maintenance that reuses the old rows' values saves little beyond exact content
reuse; scoring canonical units removes boundary drift by construction. Hosted models and content-defined units for
documents remain to be tested (Sec.~\ref{sec:disc}).
